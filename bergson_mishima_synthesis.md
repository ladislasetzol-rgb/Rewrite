## I. Bergson's Anatomy of Effort

In Chapter 1 of *Time and Free Will*, Henri Bergson meticulously dissects the sensation of muscular effort. He identifies a crucial flaw in how humans perceive physical exertion. We tend to think of muscular effort as an internal, quantitative "intensity" (a buildup of psychic force). 

Bergson argues that this is an illusion. The feeling of muscular effort is actually **extensive**, not intensive. When we feel like we are exerting "more" effort, we are actually just feeling the physical sensation spreading across a larger number of peripheral muscles. 

Bergson creates a strict dichotomy:
1.  **Deep-Seated Psychic States:** Pure consciousness, deep emotions, and abstract thought. These are qualitative and non-spatial.
2.  **Surface Sensations:** Muscular effort and physical sensations. These are tied to the physical world, space, and extension.

If a human is trapped entirely in the "deep-seated psychic states" without a physical anchor, their consciousness becomes disconnected from physical reality. In the Sovereign Architecture, we call this **Intellectual Frailty**.

## II. Mishima's Sun and Steel (The Cure for Corrosive Words)

Yukio Mishima’s *Sun and Steel* identifies the exact existential terror of being trapped in Bergson's "deep-seated psychic states." 

Mishima recognized that living entirely in the intellect (a life dominated by "words") was deeply corrosive. Language and abstract thought alienated him from physical reality, eating away at his existence and turning his body into a weak, neglected vessel. Mishima realized that pure intellect without a physical anchor leads to decay.

His solution was the **Muscular Philosophy** ("Steel"). By forcing his body through intense, grueling physical mastery (weightlifting and martial arts), Mishima forcefully bridged the gap between consciousness and the body. He used the exact **"extensive" muscular effort** Bergson described to pull his consciousness out of the abstract void and lock it into the harsh, undeniable reality of the physical world.
