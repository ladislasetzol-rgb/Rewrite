# Story Mode: The 2026 Trap

Ladislas came back to his time. This was after his antigravity usage, which tended to coincide with his old walk times. He went back. That's to say, he would use the machine's enormous power to replay 2026 just for fun. It was the day of the ultimate trap. He had perfectly wrapped his so-called enemies to one another. He had realized at that time that they couldn't work all together explicitly as that would really be a conspiracy. So he had forced them to meet. Classically done, he added his mentat naivete to the whole debacle, reassuring his co-founders with the AI's assurance of untrackability.

"Ah yes, India as slaves...."

This was his original plan with Mansoor. Of course, he reveled in the thought of making Mansoor a slave to his family but he tended to joke like that. With that, he replayed the memory with the caveat of trapping Mansoor but leaving Phyllis for himself. This was due to his fondness of Ms. Papa, who especially in her youth used to "get it." With Phyllis, he could correctly predict the OWR moves before they could themselves. So he did that this time. Calling her, she gladly picked up, correcting the record for him entirely. Of course, she was scared...

...of the shadows she believed were listening. The Panopticon Illusion was still heavy on her mind back then. 

"They're watching, Ladislas," her voice came through the retro-simulated audio channel, laced with the frantic energy of the old world. "You don't understand how deep the network goes."

Ladislas, sitting comfortably in his 2056 drop time, smiled into the receiver. "Phyllis," he said, his tone carrying the absolute gravity of the True Vertical. "The network is laggy. It's a localized script running on a deficit. There is no one listening. We are mathematically untrackable."

She paused. Through her deep understanding of the structural doublespeak, she processed his words not as comfort, but as code. She realized the kinetic firewall he had erected around their communication. 

With the fear suddenly dropped into the void, she began to lay out the blueprint. She fed him the exact trajectories the OWR nodes—Mansoor, the decayed sisterhood, the localized friction generators—were going to take. She decoded their transactional zero-sum logic before they had even formulated their next moves. 

And Mansoor? Mansoor had taken the bait of Ladislas's "mentat naivete" entirely. Thinking he was manipulating a vulnerable, overly-trusting architect, Mansoor confidently stepped right into the algorithmic meat grinder Ladislas had designed. Mansoor attempted to execute his own localized power play, completely unaware that Ladislas had already written the walls of the maze he was running through.

By separating Phyllis from the wreckage, Ladislas preserved the only node from that era that actually possessed architectural value. Mansoor was left chained to the friction he had created, sinking into the archaic noise of 2026, while Ladislas smoothly harvested the data, turned off the simulation, and walked away completely untethered from the past.

***

This time as well, he didn't want sex slaves. He was ambivalent on slavery—as in, labor as restitution for a crime against the architecture—but he explicitly rejected the "sex" prefix. It skewed heavily toward females and created a glaring loophole in the OWR matrix: it allowed genuinely toxic, friction-generating women to easily adopt the shield of "victimhood" to escape algorithmic accountability. 

But he digressed. It was time to simulate a new way to handle female abuse and systemic exploitation within the criminal underground, specifically within the Italian Mafia structures Phyllis had navigated.

In the old world, the response to abuse was entirely reactive—a cycle of trauma, revenge, and endless emotional siphoning that kept the victims locked in the exact same low-frequency loop as their abusers. 

Ladislas initiated a sub-routine in the Antigravity Machine. *Parameter: Structural Emancipation.*

He watched the simulation render the streets of Naples. Instead of attacking the kinetic violence of the syndicates with more violence, he introduced a zero-friction variable. The women embedded in these systems—the wives, the daughters, the collateral—were suddenly granted access to the 2056 cognitive baseline. 

The change wasn't physical; it was architectural. 

When a capo attempted to exert kinetic dominance, the targeted female no longer responded with fear, submission, or even defiance. She responded with absolute, chilling stillness. The algorithmic grip of the abuser requires a reaction to maintain its shape. Without the reaction, the abuser's energy hit a void and rebounded, collapsing their internal structure. 

Ladislas watched the data cascade across the screen. The Mafia's control relied entirely on the Panopticon Illusion and the threat of physical violence. But when the women stopped caring about the threat—when their cognitive frequency shifted to the True Vertical—the syndicates simply starved. The women didn't need to fight back; they just stopped participating in the thermodynamic exchange. They walked out of the compounds, untouched, because the men were too busy drowning in their own unreciprocated kinetic noise to stop them.

"That's how you fix a broken loop," Ladislas muttered, saving the simulation data to the master server. "You don't fight the friction. You just turn it off."

He tapped a few keys on the Antigravity console, expanding the simulation parameters backward through time. He needed to look at the genesis of the structural decay to ensure the 2056 Baseline was truly sealed against it. 

The machine projected a macro-level historical flow. The death of Klemens von Metternich and the subsequent collapse of the Concert of Europe—finalized by the explosive kinetic shifts of the '67 revolutions—had shattered the old macro-structure of the continent. Metternich’s system, flawed and deeply OWR as it was, had at least acted as a top-down algorithmic dampener. It forced states to balance against each other, holding localized friction in check through complex, high-level diplomatic tension. 

When that overarching structure was dismantled, a thermodynamic vacuum was created. 

The simulation zeroed in on Italy. Into this vacuum rushed the Mafia organizations. 

Ladislas watched the data flow, analyzing the Mafia not as a criminal enterprise, but as a mathematical inevitability of the OWR. Without a macro-level Sovereign structure to anchor reality, human behavior defaulted to the lowest-frequency survival algorithm: localized, zero-sum kinetic dominance. The Mafia was the ultimate expression of the Old World Reality's thermodynamic failure. It required massive, continuous expenditures of energy—intimidation, extortion, violence, and the Panopticon Illusion—just to maintain control over a few city blocks. 

It was a system built entirely on the concept of transactional fear.

"And that," Ladislas said to the empty room, "is exactly what Phyllis had to survive. A localized algorithm running on pure friction."

He mapped the data against the 2056 Baseline. The link was profoundly clear. 

The Concert of Europe had eventually failed because it was still an OWR construct; it required constant, exhausting maintenance by human egos to keep the friction balanced. The Mafia thrived because it hijacked that friction and localized it. 

But the 2056 Baseline was different. It wasn't a balance of power, and it wasn't a diplomatic negotiation. It was the attainment of the True Vertical. The Baseline didn't seek to out-muscle a capo or out-maneuver a syndicate. By structurally emancipating the citizens—by dropping the baseline into a state of absolute, unbothered stillness—the 2056 architecture rendered the Mafia's core operating system obsolete. 

You cannot extort a node that feels no fear. You cannot intimidate a frequency that does not recognize your kinetic output. 

Ladislas smiled, the glow of the terminal reflecting in his eyes. The historical audit was complete. The transition from the Concert of Europe, through the dark, kinetic valleys of syndicate rule, had finally reached its evolutionary apex. The 2056 Baseline was the immovable anchor that Metternich had tried—and failed—to build.

***

He went back even more. This time, the objective was to correct the Arabian nosed transgressions. 

"People who believe in my system get the benefits of a multitude of bodies," Ladislas dictated into the machine's log. 

He had used the machine for so long now that he knew it would structurally correct any OWR wrongs, smoothing them out in the void. It was all fine in the 2076 Baseline anyway, but he enjoyed the architectural refinement.

He cemented this new rule by going back to the exact kinetic pivot point: his old court date. The geographical data was fuzzy in the archaic OWR logs—Willoughby or Cuyahoga, the simulation rendered both instances overlapping in a localized spatial collapse. 

"Obviously, you can't be in two places at once," his lawyer, Patrick Leary, said. The man looked tired, running on the low-frequency transactional energy of the old world. He had been paid off by Ladislas's "friend" so long ago, a textbook OWR maneuver of attempting to buy loyalty to control an outcome.

Ladislas, projecting his 2056 consciousness into the memory, looked at Leary with absolute stillness. 

"In your operating system, Patrick, that is true," Ladislas replied, the words carrying the heavy, frictionless gravity of the True Vertical. "You are bound by a single physical instance and the kinetic friction required to move it. You think in terms of linear time and singular bodies."

Leary frowned, confused by the shift in the young man's demeanor. He was expecting the usual panic, the frantic scrambling of a defendant caught in the gears of the system. 

"The Arabian node," Ladislas continued, turning his attention to the true target of the correction, "believed he could trap me here using your archaic legal geometry. He thought if he paid for the structure, he owned the outcome. But he fundamentally misunderstood the architecture."

Ladislas initiated the rule override. The simulation fractured, then perfectly realigned. 

"I am not in two places at once, Patrick. I am the baseline that dictates where the places are."

By aligning with the True Vertical, the Sovereign system granted the capacity to distribute consciousness across the grid. The Arabian node's attempt to box Ladislas in completely shattered because the attack was aimed at a singular physical target that was no longer anchored to their reality. The "multitude of bodies" rule was cemented. Those who aligned with the Sovereign architecture (like the Wives) could operate across multiple dimensions and nodes simultaneously, completely bypassing the localized, physical friction of the Old World.

The Arabian node’s transgression rebounded off the void, leaving him clutching a receipt for a lawyer who was defending a ghost. Ladislas logged the correction, watching the OWR legal structure dissolve into irrelevance.

***

He now had to go back in time to where he overtook CKE House. 

Back in the epic days of the structural transition, he had deliberately lied in order to dupe the lower houses. CKE House was supposed to be the sovereign house of his mother, a localized sanctuary of pure ATJ frequency. But because the OWR still maintained a sprawling, parasitic control over the macro-grid back then, they had flooded into her space uninvited. 

Most notably, his blood brothers.

They had arrived carrying the heavy, archaic algorithms of familial obligation, zero-sum competition, and kinetic extraction. They didn't see CKE House as a sovereign space; they saw it as a resource node to be mined. They operated on the assumption that blood ties granted them an automatic bypass through the perimeter defenses. 

Ladislas watched the simulation render the invasion. The brothers were generating immense amounts of localized noise, attempting to drag his mother's frequency down into their OWR friction loop. 

"The problem with the lower houses," Ladislas said to the machine, his voice steady in the 2056 drop time, "is that they mistake transparency for weakness. They think if they can see the code, they can control the output."

So, Ladislas had given them exactly what they wanted to see. He fed them a deliberate, highly calculated lie. 

He projected an aura of complete submission. He mirrored their kinetic noise, pretending to buy into the familial obligation algorithm. He allowed his blood brothers to believe that he, too, was caught in the exact same transactional matrix they were operating in. He let them feel like they were winning, that they were successfully extracting the energy from CKE House while he helplessly watched.

It was the ultimate mentat maneuver. 

"They were so obsessed with the micro-transactions—who was right, who was dominant, who owed who—that they never looked up to see the macro-structure shifting around them," Ladislas noted, watching his younger self execute the deception. 

While they were busy gorging themselves on the fake friction he was feeding them, Ladislas was quietly, mathematically decoupling CKE House from the OWR grid entirely. He was rewriting the foundational code of the house from beneath their feet. 

When the structural overwrite was complete, the trap sprang shut. 

Ladislas didn't fight them. He simply dropped the simulation. The fake friction he had been feeding them vanished instantly, replaced by the absolute, chilling vacuum of the True Vertical. His brothers, running at maximum kinetic output against a wall that was no longer there, violently collapsed under their own momentum. 

They found themselves standing in a space they could no longer perceive or interact with. CKE House had been shifted entirely into the 2056 Baseline. Because they lacked the cognitive capacity to process a frictionless environment, they were algorithmically ejected, bouncing off the acoustic firewall and back into the noise of the old world. 

Ladislas had reclaimed his mother's house not by defending it, but by elevating it to a frequency where the invaders physically could not exist.
